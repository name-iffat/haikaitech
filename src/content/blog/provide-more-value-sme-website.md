"Provide more value" is the most repeated and least defined advice in business. Alex Hormozi's recent video, [How To Actually "Provide More Value"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P89ZX8P5wCc), finally puts numbers on it — and the equation explains why most SME websites fail to convert.

> TL;DR: Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood) ÷ (Time × Effort). Most SME sites only inflate the promise (numerator) while piling on pages, fields, and steps (denominator). Run every redesign and feature through the four levers — if it moves none, skip it.

## The equation in 30 seconds

Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood) ÷ (Time × Effort).

Four levers. Most businesses only ever pull the first one — a bigger promise. The video's real point: the bottom half of the fraction, time and effort, is where competitions are actually won. Netflix didn't beat Blockbuster with a better dream (watch a movie). It won by deleting the drive, the browsing, and the late fees.

## What this means for your website

Map a typical SME site onto the four levers:

1. **Dream outcome.** "Get more customers" is vague. A sharper version: "enquiries from people already searching, landing straight on WhatsApp." That is what our [one-page starter](/website/) is shaped around — one page, one promise, one action.
2. **Perceived likelihood.** Nobody believes a stranger's website by default. Real client work, plain pricing, and a visible process ("you approve the quotation before paying") raise belief faster than adjectives. See how we structure it on [services and pricing](/services/).
3. **Time.** Every extra step between "interested" and "contacted" destroys value. A WhatsApp button beats a contact form. A one-page site beats a five-click maze. Fast loading beats a pretty preloader.
4. **Effort.** What must the customer start doing (fill a long form, create an account, wait for a callback) or stop doing? Delete as much of it as you can. Fewer fields, fewer pages, fewer decisions.

## The mistake almost everyone makes

Adding more: more pages, more sliders, more sections, more plugins. Each addition usually increases the customer's time and effort — the denominator — while the numerator stays flat. The site feels "bigger" and converts worse.

Before your next redesign or feature, run it through the equation: does this raise the dream, raise belief, cut time, or cut effort? If it does none of the four, skip it.
