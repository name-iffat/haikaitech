A website backlog grows quickly. New logo. New homepage. Booking flow. Blog. Meanwhile, the WhatsApp button may still point to the wrong number.

Alex Hormozi's MoreMozi video ["Which Task Has the Most Impact?"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4IWsB8bmPxQ) uses impact and effort to sort a long task list. For a small business website, start with changes that help a real customer understand the offer or take the next step. The rest can wait.

## Check the customer path first

Open your site on a phone and try to use it as a new customer. Can you tell what the business does, who it serves and how to contact it? Tap the WhatsApp button. Send a test enquiry. Check that the phone number, message and any form all work.

These checks are plain, but they catch the kind of problem a new animation won't fix. If the contact route is broken or the service details are vague, tidy that up before planning a full redesign.

Google's [SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide) recommends useful, readable, well-organised content. That is a good test for the page too: can someone scan it and find the information they came for?

## Sort the next tasks by impact and effort

Write down the changes you are considering. For each one, ask: will this make it easier for a customer to understand, trust or contact the business? How much time and money will it take?

Start with the useful changes that are easy to finish:

- Correct a broken WhatsApp link, phone number or email address.
- Put the main service and service area in plain language.
- Make the main contact action easy to spot.
- Replace placeholder text or outdated details.

Then decide whether larger work is worth scoping. A booking or payment flow may help if customers genuinely need it. A second landing page may make sense if the business serves a different audience. Neither needs to be first just because a competitor has one.

A new logo, extra animation or another section can be nice. But if customers still cannot tell what you do, those are probably not the first jobs to fund.

## You may not need a full redesign

Some sites need rebuilding. Others need a few careful fixes. Check the current one before choosing: test the contact route, read the key page on a phone, and ask someone outside the business to find the service and contact details. If they get stuck, note where. That gives you a more useful brief than "make it look modern."

## Common questions

### What should I fix first on my business website?
Start with anything that blocks a customer from understanding the service or contacting you. Check the wording, phone details and main contact button before adding new features.

### Do I need to redesign the whole website?
Not always. Fix broken links and unclear information first. Consider a redesign when the current structure makes important information hard to find or maintain.

### Should I add online booking or payment?
Only if it solves a real customer or operations problem. Write down the steps people need to take and get the scope confirmed before paying for a custom flow.

### Will a better website guarantee more enquiries?
No. A website cannot guarantee enquiries or search rankings. Your offer, traffic and follow-up matter too.
