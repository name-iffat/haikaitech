Every week, a business owner in Shah Alam or Petaling Jaya opens Google, searches "web designer Selangor", and gets back a confusing mix of freelancers, agencies and platforms — with prices from RM500 to RM50,000. This post is a checklist for making that decision well, whether your designer is in Selangor, Penang, or anywhere else in Malaysia.

## The location question, settled

Here is a truth that saves most people hours of anxiety: **for a business website, where your designer is physically located barely matters.**

We are a Penang-based studio serving clients across Malaysia, including Selangor. It works because everything happens remotely — scoping calls, reviews on a staging link, and handover. We share the same timezone (that matters more than the same city), and WhatsApp is the working channel.

So treat "must be in Selangor" as a nice-to-have, not a requirement. What actually matters is on this checklist.

## The checklist

### 1. Do they publish real prices?

Useful pricing pages explain both the starting point and why the final number changes. Our [services and pricing page](/services/) shows an introductory one-page starter from RM799 alongside the factors that move a formal quotation. Vague "contact us for price" pages make comparison impossible — skip them.

### 2. Who owns the website?

For completed one-time quoted projects with us, the agreed credentials, source and deliverables are handed over after final payment. Whoever you hire, get this in writing: after the final payment, do you hold the domain, the hosting access, and the site files?

### 3. What happens after launch?

Websites need small updates — a changed price, a new photo, a promo headline. Ask whether updates are included, charged per request, or bundled yearly. Our starter includes two minor content updates after launch; [larger maintenance needs](/services/) are quoted separately.

### 4. Is basic SEO actually included?

At minimum, every page should ship with a proper title, description, canonical URL and sitemap — the unglamorous basics that make you indexable. Ask for the list. If "SEO" in the quote means a plugin was installed, that is not the same thing.

### 5. How do they handle content?

Most delays come from content, not code. A good designer tells you exactly what to provide (logo, text, photos you have permission to use) and confirms who writes or sources what in the quotation.

### 6. Do they promise customers or rankings?

No enquiry volume or search ranking can be guaranteed by a build. Promises of either are a red flag; honest designers say so up front.
