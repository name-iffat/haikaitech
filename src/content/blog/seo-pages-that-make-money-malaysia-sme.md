Most SME blogs are dead weight. Someone pays a writer for "5 tips for stronger customer relationships", it ranks for a week, then Google answers the question directly and nobody clicks through.

Edward Sturm made this point bluntly in his recent video, [SEO that makes money...](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X7llRL6Mycw). His argument: stop spending hours on informational articles. Build bottom-of-funnel pages instead. Pages that target keywords typed by people who are ready to buy, not people who are just browsing.

> TL;DR: Informational posts are losing clicks to AI answers (Ahrefs: top-result CTR down ~58% where AI Overviews appear). Build bottom-of-funnel pages instead — service plus area, price pages, contact paths ending at WhatsApp — because those match buyers with money in hand.

The numbers back him up. Ahrefs ran a large study on AI Overviews and found clicks to the top result dropped by about a third when an AI answer appeared, and their December 2025 update puts the drop even higher. You can read their full breakdown here: [AI Overviews reduce clicks by 58%](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-reduce-clicks-update/). Informational content took the hit. Pages with buying intent held up far better.

## What this means for a Malaysian SME

Think about how your own customers search. Nobody finds a contractor by reading "the history of cement". They search things like "kontraktor bumbung bocor penang" or "harga buat website malaysia". Those searches come from people with a problem and money in hand.

So the pages worth building are simple:

A services page that says what you do and where. One service, one area, one page. "Aircond service Shah Alam" beats a generic "our services" page every time.

A price page. Most Malaysian SMEs hide their prices and wonder why enquiries go nowhere. A page that states your starting price filters out window shoppers and pulls in serious buyers. Our own breakdown of [how much a business website costs in Malaysia](/blog/how-much-does-a-business-website-cost-in-malaysia/) works exactly this way. It answers the money question first.

A contact path with zero friction. Every one of these pages should end at WhatsApp, not a five-field form. The person searching "harga" wants a number today, not a callback next Tuesday.

## Common questions

### Should I delete my old blog posts?

Not necessarily. Leave them up if they cost you nothing. Just stop spending new effort there until your money pages exist and rank.

### What is a bottom-of-funnel page?

A page for someone who already wants to buy. Service plus area, pricing, package comparison, "near me" style pages. The visitor is choosing a supplier, not learning a topic.

### Can a one-page website rank for these keywords?

It can rank for one main keyword cluster, like your service plus your town. If you cover many areas or services, you will eventually need more pages. Start with the one that pays first.

### Do I still need Google or Facebook ads?

Ads buy speed. Pages buy durability. If your budget is tight, build the pages first so ad traffic also has somewhere good to land.

### How fast does this work?

New pages typically take weeks to months to rank. That is normal. The point is that once they rank, each visitor is worth far more than a blog reader.
