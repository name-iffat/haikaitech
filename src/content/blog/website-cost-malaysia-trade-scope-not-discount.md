# Website Cost Malaysia: Trade Scope Instead of Begging for Discount

This post started from a short Alex Hormozi clip, ["Have More Things to Trade Than Just the Price"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F2xmBkzLXKg). His point is simple. If price is the only thing on the table, every negotiation has a loser. The fix is to break the deal into smaller pieces so both sides can trade without touching the price.

I think Malaysian SME owners need this more than most. Our opening move is always "boleh kurang?" I have done it myself. But on a website job, that move usually backfires.

## Why "boleh kurang" fails on websites

A website quote is mostly labour. When you push the price down and change nothing else, the studio has two options. Earn less per hour, or quietly put in fewer hours. Nobody tells you which one they picked. You find out three months later when the copy is thin, the photos are stock, and nobody updates anything.

Hormozi's answer is to put more variables on the table. Speed, risk, effort, scope, payment. Trade those instead of the number. I like this because it works on both sides of the table.

## What you can actually trade

Content is the biggest one. If you write your own text and supply real photos of your shop, team and work, you just removed hours of chasing and guessing. That is worth more than a 10 percent discount, and any honest studio will admit it.

Scope is next. A one page site with five tight sections beats a ten page site where seven pages say nothing. Fewer sections, done well, costs less because there is less to build and less to go wrong.

Then timeline. If you are not in a rush, say so. A flexible deadline is a real concession. Rush work costs more everywhere, not just in web design.

Payment terms count too. A fast deposit and fast feedback are worth real money to a small studio. Chasing clients for content and balance payments is where small projects go to die.

And self editing. If you do not need to edit the site yourself, do not pay for a CMS you will never open. Let the studio handle updates. It is cheaper upfront.

## Questions people always ask

**Why won't studios just give a small discount?**

Some will. But a small studio discounting a small project is giving away margin it does not have. I would rather a studio say no to the discount and yes to adjusting scope. That honesty tells you how they will treat you after launch.

**What is the best thing to trade for a lower quote?**

Content readiness. Real text, real photos, fast decisions. It shortens the project more than anything else. Second best is cutting pages and sections you do not need yet.

**Is DIY cheaper than hiring someone?**

Upfront, usually yes. Indicative DIY builder costs run in the low hundreds per year, but you pay with your own weekends and the site still needs words and photos from you. If your time earns more doing your actual business, DIY is the expensive option.

**My budget is genuinely tight. What should I do?**

Say the number out loud and ask what scope fits it. A one page Starter that brings enquiries beats a big site you cannot afford to finish. You can add pages later when the site pays for itself.
