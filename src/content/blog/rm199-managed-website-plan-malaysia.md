Most Malaysian business owners do not want to become website administrators. They want a website that stays available, remains secure and can be updated when the business changes. The important question is not only what the build costs, but what happens after launch.

## What ownership should mean

For a completed one-time quoted project, the quotation should identify what is handed over after final payment. Depending on the project, that may include:

- Domain or DNS access,
- Hosting access,
- Website administration credentials,
- Source files or repository access,
- Design and content deliverables,
- Documentation for custom systems.

Third-party software, stock assets, fonts, plugins and platforms may carry their own licences. Those terms should be listed separately rather than implied to be owned outright.

## The costs that continue after launch

Even a one-time build can have ongoing costs:

| Cost | Why it exists |
|---|---|
| Domain renewal | Keeps the website address registered |
| Hosting | Keeps the website or system available online |
| Paid plugins or tools | Covers licensed functionality used by the project |
| Provider usage | Covers email, storage, APIs, automation or other metered services |
| Maintenance | Covers updates, monitoring and technical fixes where required |
| Content changes | Covers new pages, sections, products, copy or imagery after approval |

The formal quotation should identify known recurring and third-party costs. Where a provider controls the final fee, the quotation should state that the amount can change under that provider's terms.

## Maintenance is different from a new feature

Maintenance keeps an agreed build working. A new feature changes what the website or system does. Updating dependencies, renewing infrastructure and fixing an existing form are maintenance tasks; adding a new booking flow, member portal or integration is new development.

Separating the two avoids a common disagreement: one side expects unlimited improvements while the other priced only routine upkeep.

## Questions to ask before signing

1. What credentials and files are handed over after completion?
2. Which domain, hosting and software costs recur?
3. Who owns each account used by the website?
4. What support is included immediately after launch?
5. How are future updates and new features quoted?
6. What happens if you move the website to another provider?
