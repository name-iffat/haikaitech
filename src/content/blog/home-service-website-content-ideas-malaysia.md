Home service owners ask the same thing before ordering a website. Aircond, plumbing, wiring, cleaning. What pages do I actually need? Paying for pages nobody reads is a waste.

Google already shows what customers type. That is the whole trick in Edward Sturm's buildinpublic video ["Google is giving home service companies content ideas for free"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pCE_fvH3ryQ). My Malaysian SME take below uses his idea without copying his script. Type, read, and plan.

> TL;DR: Type your service plus your area into Google, read the autocomplete and "People also ask" questions, and group them into a page plan — price questions to FAQ, problem questions to the service description, area questions to coverage. One strong page first; add pages only when real customer questions overflow it.

## Start with the search box

Open Google in a normal browser window. Type your service plus your area. Try "aircond service selangor", "tukang paip shah alam", or "wiring rumah kos". Do not press Enter yet. Read the autocomplete list. Those are real phrases people typed before you.

Press Enter. Scroll past the ads. Note the "People also ask" questions and the related searches at the bottom. Write down five to eight questions that match jobs you actually do. Ignore the rest.

Group them. Price questions go to one FAQ block. Problem questions like "aircond leaking water" go near the service description. Area questions like "cover klang tak" go to a short coverage section. This grouped list is your page plan.

## One clear page beats ten thin ones

My opinion: most home service SMEs need one strong service page first. Service, coverage area, price approach, how to book, contact. Add a second page only when a new group of questions cannot fit cleanly on the first.

Thin pages hurt you twice. They cost money to build and they confuse customers. A customer who finds the price logic, the booking steps, and the phone number on one page will contact you faster than one who clicks through five empty pages.

If the question list keeps growing after three months of real enquiries, that is the time to add pages. Each new page should answer one group of questions fully.

## What this means for website cost

The question list sets the scope. A short list fits Starter at RM799. That is one page, 5 sections, 2 updates, 1 year hosting plus domain. A longer list with regular updates fits Professional plus Lite CMS at RM1,700, or Full CMS at RM3,300 if you publish often. Online ordering needs WooCommerce at RM3,500. Dashboards or ops tools are custom software from RM5,000.

So do the Google check before you ask for a quote. Bring the list. It keeps the quote honest on both sides.

**Key takeaway:** Five to eight real customer questions from Google beat five thin pages. Build the one page that answers them, then let three months of real enquiries tell you what page two should be.

## Common questions

### Do I need many pages to rank?
No. One page that answers the customer's question clearly is better than five pages with two lines each. Add pages when real customer questions overflow the current page.

### Does this method guarantee ranking or enquiries?
No. It shows demand in your own words. Ranking still needs a fast site, correct business info, reviews, and follow-up. Anyone who promises a top spot is guessing.

### My trade has few searches. Still useful?
Yes. Fewer searches means each enquiry matters more. Five exact questions from Google can shape a page that converts the few callers you get.

### Can I do this without a website?
You can collect the list now and use it in WhatsApp replies and quotes. But the list works best on a website where customers can read it at midnight without messaging you.

### How often should I update the list?
Check every few months or when customers start asking something new. Update the page text, test the WhatsApp button, and check that prices and coverage are still right.
