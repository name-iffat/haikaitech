Google is rolling out another core update right now. It started on 10 November 2026 and takes about two weeks, so rankings are still moving as I write this.

That timing matters. If your traffic looks weird this week, do not panic yet. Google itself says to wait until the rollout finishes before judging anything. Anyone selling you an emergency fix mid-rollout is selling too early.

Google calls it a regular "relevant, satisfying content" update, not a war on AI content; but unreviewed bulk-AI sites are exactly the pages reporting drops.

## What people are reporting

Edward Sturm covered the early reports in his video, [Google's November Core Update Is Crushing AI Content Sites](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kroDk8LuKtY). His summary, in short: several sites that scaled up AI-generated articles fast are showing steep drops. He cites a site owner who rebuilt with Claude Code and says clicks fell from around 200 a day to 20. Another site publishing roughly one AI article a day declined sharply. He also retells an older experiment where two SEOs published 1,200 AI articles, saw traffic grow, then watched it collapse months later.

Treat these as reported anecdotes, not verified data. They come from site owners talking during a rollout, and Search Console data has been laggy for some sites in recent weeks. Still, the pattern is worth noticing.

Google's own description is calmer. It calls this a regular update meant to surface relevant, satisfying content, and says core updates do not target specific sites. So the honest framing is this: Google did not announce a war on AI content, but sites full of unreviewed, thin AI articles look like exactly the kind of pages a "satisfying content" update would demote.

## What to do if your traffic dropped

My take, borrowing from both Google's guidance and Edward's video:

Wait first. The rollout should finish around 24 November. Compare a full week after that against the week before 10 November. Anything you conclude mid-rollout is guesswork.

Then check which pages fell. If it is the whole site, that is a bigger signal than two or three posts dipping. Search the queries you lost and read what ranks now. Usually the winners answer the question faster, show real experience, or just look written by someone who has done the work.

Fix those pages properly. Update stale info, add examples from your own jobs, cut filler. Do not just rephrase the same thin article and republish it. And keep publishing the pages that bring enquiries, not more informational posts. If AI helps you draft, fine, but a human who knows the business should review every page before it goes live. Unreviewed bulk publishing is the exact habit this update seems to punish.

## Common questions

### Did Google confirm it is targeting AI content?

No. Google described the November 2026 update as a regular update to surface relevant, satisfying content. The AI-content casualties are anecdotal reports from site owners so far.

### Should I delete my AI-written posts?

Not in a panic. Wait until the rollout ends, see which pages actually lost traffic, and then decide page by page. Improve or remove the ones that are thin. Leave anything that still brings enquiries alone.

### Can AI still be part of my content process?

Yes, as a drafting helper. The risky pattern is publishing AI output at scale with no review. A human who knows the business should check facts, add real examples, and cut generic filler before anything goes live.

### My traffic dropped this week. Is it the update?

Maybe, but you cannot know yet. Rankings move during rollouts and Search Console has been unreliable for some sites lately. Compare full weeks after the rollout finishes before concluding anything.

### Where should a small budget go instead?

Bottom-of-funnel pages: services, pricing, and one service-plus-area page for your best job type. Those match what buyers search and hold up better through updates than generic blog posts.
